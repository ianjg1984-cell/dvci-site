Language Itself Well Documented

Dog Days

"The hottest, most sultry part of summer."

Share on Facebook

Nothing to do with dogs being hot and bothered. According to Wikipedia, the English term is a calque, a word-for-word loan translation, of the Latin dies caniculares ("the puppy days"), itself modelled on the ancient Greek kynades hemerai. The "dog" is the star Sirius, the Dog Star, and the dog days were historically the period following Sirius's heliacal rising, when it first becomes visible again just before sunrise, in the hottest part of summer. The phrase is in English by the 16th century: a 1564 book called Hope of Health uses "Dogge daies," and 16th-century English liturgies observed canonical "dog daies" from 7 July to 5 September.

Sources: Wikipedia, 'Dog days' (calque of Latin dies caniculares and Greek kynades hemerai; the 1564 Hope of Health; 16th-century English liturgies).
