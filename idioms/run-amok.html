Run Amok

"To go berserk, or behave in a wild, uncontrolled way."

Share on Facebook

From Malay amuk, "rushing in a frenzy" or, in Wiktionary's gloss, "to go on a killing spree," borrowed into English via Portuguese amouco. The word is associated with the people of Malaysia and Java and appears in the Portuguese Book of Duarte Barbosa (c. 1516-18), which says "These are called Amuco." Wikipedia's list of English words of Malay origin gives the earliest known English use as 1665, as a noun for a Malay in a homicidal frenzy. A story that "amok" comes from sailors running a ship aground is a false etymology.

Sources: Duarte Barbosa, Book of Duarte Barbosa (c. 1516-18); Wikipedia's 'List of English words of Malay origin' (earliest known English use, 1665); Wiktionary and Mental Floss on the Malay and Portuguese forms.
