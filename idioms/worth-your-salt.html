Worth Your Salt

"Good enough at a job to deserve your pay."

Share on Facebook

The popular explanation is that Roman soldiers were paid in salt, and that this is why we have both the word "salary" and the phrase "worth your salt." Latin salarium does come from sal, "salt," but Wikipedia describes the exact connection as unknown, calls the claim that Roman legions were sometimes paid in salt "baseless," and says even a salt allowance isn't supported by ancient sources. The idiom itself is far younger than Rome. Wiktionary gives its first attestation as 1786, in Alexander Dalrymple's pamphlet about the East India Company and its shipowners, which refers to "all the Shipwrights that will ever be worth their salt!" The phrase may simply play on salt having been a fairly valuable commodity in pre-modern times, with or without a nod to the word "salary."

Myth vs. FactThe Roman soldiers-paid-in-salt story is repeated almost everywhere, but Wikipedia's article on salary calls it baseless. The idiom itself is first attested in 1786, according to Wiktionary.
Sources: Alexander Dalrymple, A Fair State of the Case between the East India Company and the Owners of Ships Now in Their Service (1786), first attestation per Wiktionary; the Roman salt-pay claim assessed in Wikipedia's 'Salary' article and in Peter Gainsford's 2017 article 'Salt and salary: were Roman soldiers paid in salt?', cited by Wiktionary.
