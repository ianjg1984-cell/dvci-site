Sailing & the Sea Well Documented

Hand Over Fist

"Very quickly, especially when making money."

Share on Facebook

A sailors' phrase from hauling rope. Phrase Finder says it is probably of naval origin, from the action of hauling on a rope, and that it grew out of the earlier "hand over hand." That form is recorded in 1736 in a piece by Cooke in the Royal Society's Philosophical Transactions: "A lusty young Man attempted to go down (hand over hand, as the Workmen call it)." William Falconer's An Universal Dictionary of the Marine (1769) defines an order to pull on a rope hand-over-hand. The "fist" version appears in William Glascock's The Naval Sketch-Book (1825): "coming up with us, 'hand over fist', in three divisions." The money sense, rapid gain, is recorded in Seba Smith's The Life and Writings of Major Jack Downing (1833), in which people "clawed the money off of his table, hand over fist."

Sources: Phrase Finder on 'hand over fist' (Cooke, Philosophical Transactions, 1736; William Falconer, An Universal Dictionary of the Marine, 1769; William Glascock, The Naval Sketch-Book, 1825; Seba Smith, Major Jack Downing, 1833).
