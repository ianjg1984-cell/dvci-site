Hoist by Your Own Petard

"To be undone by your own scheme or trick."

Share on Facebook

This one is a direct Shakespeare coinage, from Hamlet: "For 'tis the sport to have the engineer hoist with his own petard." A petard was a real, primitive explosive device, a small bomb packed into a metal or wooden case, used to blow open city gates and castle walls. "Hoist" is an old form of "hoise," meaning lifted or blown upward. Put together, the image is of a bomb-maker blown into the air by his own bomb, close-in irony rather than distant misfortune. There's also a buried joke: "petard" comes from French pétard, ultimately from a Latin verb meaning "to break wind," so Shakespeare's audience likely caught a second, cruder meaning too.

Sources: William Shakespeare, Hamlet (c.1600), Act III, Scene IV; etymology of 'petard' traced through French and Latin in the Oxford English Dictionary.
