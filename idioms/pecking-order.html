Language Itself Well Documented

Pecking Order

"A ranking of status within a group, from top to bottom."

Share on Facebook

A zoology term that became a social one. The Norwegian zoologist Thorleif Schjelderup-Ebbe described the dominance hierarchy among hens, which he called Hackliste, later rendered in German as Hackordnung. Sources differ on when: Wikipedia credits a 1913 paper, "The Voices of Chickens," with first describing it, while Phrase Finder points to his 1921 doctoral dissertation. The English "pecking order" is recorded in K. C. Creasy's 1927 English translation of Friedrich Alverdes's Social Life in Animal World: "Such 'pecking orders' give the society concerned a certain degree of organization." The earliest figurative use outside poultry that Phrase Finder cites is from the Pittsburgh Sun-Telegraph in October 1929, about a woman whose "position of number two in the pecking order would never have been questioned." Schjelderup-Ebbe also reported cycles within dominance structures, so the neat ladder the phrase suggests isn't quite what he found.

Sources: Phrase Finder on Schjelderup-Ebbe (1921 dissertation), K. C. Creasy's 1927 translation of Alverdes, and the Pittsburgh Sun-Telegraph (October 1929); Wikipedia's article on Schjelderup-Ebbe (1913 paper).
