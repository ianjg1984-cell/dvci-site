Sailing & the Sea Likely Origin (Disputed)

By and Large

"On the whole; in general."

Share on Facebook

A sailing phrase. In the days of square-rigged ships, a vessel sailing "by the wind" was pointed towards the wind, while sailing "large" meant having the wind from behind the beam, a freer, easier course. World Wide Words explains that "by and large" in sailors' language referred to all possible points of sailing, which is how it came to mean "in all possible circumstances" and then, in landsmen's speech, "all things considered." That last step is the author's own explanation rather than a documented change, and we couldn't verify a dated early citation for the nautical phrase, so the sailing origin is widely accepted but not nailed down.

Sources: World Wide Words, 'By and large: A Nautical Origin Explained'; no dated early citation verified.
