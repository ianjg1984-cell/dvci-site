Sailing & the Sea Likely Origin (Disputed)

Pipe Down

"To stop making noise and calm down."

Share on Facebook

A navy phrase. A ship's boatswain (bosun) blew a pipe, or whistle, to signal orders, changes of duty, and the end of the day's main work. Wikipedia's article on the boatswain's call lists "Pipe Down" among the standard calls, meaning dismissal of all the crew not on watch. From that command to stand down, the phrase broadened into the everyday request to be quiet, a shift that is easy to imagine with a crew dismissed for the night. We couldn't find a verified early printed citation for either the naval call or the figurative use, so we can't say when the change in meaning happened.

Sources: Boatswain's call commands as listed on Wikipedia; Wayword Radio on the nautical origin. No dated early citation verified.
