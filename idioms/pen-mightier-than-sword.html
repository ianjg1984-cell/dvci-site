Literature Well Documented

The Pen Is Mightier Than the Sword

"Words and ideas have more lasting power to influence people than violence or force."

Share on Facebook

This exact wording comes from English author Edward Bulwer-Lytton's 1839 play Richelieu; Or the Conspiracy, first performed at London's Covent Garden. The line belongs to Cardinal Richelieu, a man of the church who can't defend himself with a blade against a plot on his life, and instead resolves to use his writing to turn public opinion in his favour. Bulwer-Lytton wasn't working from nothing, though: similar sentiments show up much earlier, including a line in Shakespeare's Hamlet (1602) about men "afraid of goose-quills," and a 1582 line comparing the "dashe of a Pen" to being struck by a lance.

Sources: Edward Bulwer-Lytton, Richelieu; Or the Conspiracy (1839); earlier precedents in William Shakespeare's Hamlet (1602) and George Whetstone's Heptameron of Civil Discourses (1582).
