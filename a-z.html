A-Z Index

Every idiom in the repository so far (98 and counting), listed alphabetically. Tap a letter to jump straight there.

A B C D E F G H I J K L M N O P R S T U W

A

  • A cock and bull storyAn elaborate, implausible tale.
  • A skeleton in the cupboardA shameful secret kept hidden, usually within a family.
  • Argy-bargyA heated argument or dispute.
  • At a loose endHaving nothing to do; idle.
  • At sixes and sevensIn a state of confusion or disarray.

B

  • BairnA child, especially a baby or young son or daughter.
  • Baker's DozenThirteen, one more than a standard dozen.
  • Barking Up the Wrong TreeTo pursue a mistaken assumption or the wrong course of action entirely.
  • Batten down the hatchesTo prepare for a difficulty or crisis.
  • Between a Rock and a Hard PlaceCaught in a difficult situation where every available option is bad.
  • Between the devil and the deep blue seaStuck between two equally bad or difficult choices.
  • Beyond the paleOutside the bounds of acceptable behaviour.
  • Bite the bulletTo face an unpleasant or painful situation with courage.
  • Bits and bobsA miscellaneous collection of small items.
  • Bob's your uncleThere you go; it's as simple as that.
  • Bog standardOrdinary, basic, without any special features.
  • BostinExcellent; great.
  • Break a LegA traditional way to wish a performer good luck right before they go on stage.
  • Break the iceTo ease initial social tension or awkwardness, especially between strangers.
  • Bricking itTo be extremely frightened or nervous.
  • Bury the HatchetTo formally end a conflict and restore peace.
  • By hook or by crookBy whatever means necessary, fair or otherwise.
  • By the Skin of Your TeethTo narrowly escape or achieve something, by the smallest possible margin.

C

  • Catch-22A no-win situation created by contradictory rules, where the only solution is blocked by the problem itself.
  • Caught red-handedCaught in the act of doing something wrong.
  • Chip on Your ShoulderTo carry a grudge or a persistent, defensive resentment, as if spoiling for a fight.
  • Chuffed to bitsExtremely pleased or delighted.
  • CodswallopNonsense; rubbish talk.
  • Cold TurkeyTo stop a habit or addiction abruptly and completely, without weaning off gradually.
  • Cost a bombTo be very expensive.
  • Curiosity Killed the CatA warning that being too inquisitive can lead to trouble.
  • Curry favourTo ingratiate yourself with someone through flattery, especially for personal gain.
  • Cut and runTo leave quickly, especially to escape a difficult situation.
  • Cut to the ChaseTo get straight to the point, skipping unnecessary detail.
  • CwtchA hug or cuddle that carries a sense of safety and warmth, or a small cupboard.

D

  • DiehardSomeone stubbornly loyal to a cause or belief, refusing to give up even against the odds.
  • Donkey's yearsA very long time.

E

  • Elbow greaseHard physical effort, especially manual scrubbing or polishing.
  • Elbow roomEnough space to move or act freely.
  • Ey upHello; also used as an exclamation of surprise or to draw attention.

F

  • Fair clemmedVery hungry.
  • Fit as a fiddleIn excellent health or condition.

G

  • GadgieA man, especially an older bloke, sometimes used for any adult male.
  • Gan cannyTake care; go carefully.
  • GinnelA narrow alleyway or passage running between buildings, usually terraced houses.
  • Give it some wellyTo put in more effort or power, especially when driving or exerting physical force.
  • Give someone the cold shoulderTo deliberately treat someone with unfriendly indifference.
  • Gone for a BurtonLost, broken, ruined, or (originally) killed.
  • Gordon Bennett!An exclamation of surprise, frustration, or exasperation.

H

  • Have a chin wagTo have a chat, especially a long or gossipy one.

I

  • I don't like the cut of your jibTo dislike (or like) someone based on their general appearance or manner.
  • In a pickleIn a difficult, awkward, or messy situation.

J

  • JiggeredUtterly exhausted, or, as an exclamation ('well, I'll be jiggered!'), astonished.

K

  • Kick the bucketTo die.

L

  • Let Sleeping Dogs LieDon't disturb a settled situation, or you may provoke new trouble.
  • Let the cat out of the bagTo reveal a secret, often accidentally.
  • Loose CannonAn unpredictable person prone to causing damage if left unchecked.

M

  • Mad as a hatterCompletely crazy or eccentric.
  • MardySulky, moody, or irritable.
  • Mind your Ps and QsBehave properly; be on your best manners.
  • MitherTo pester, nag, or make an unnecessary fuss.

N

  • NeshOverly sensitive to cold weather; soft or feeble.
  • No room to swing a catAn extremely small or cramped space.
  • Not the full shillingNot very intelligent, or not quite right in the head.
  • Nowt and Owt"Nowt" means nothing and "owt" means anything, as in 'there's nowt so queer as folk' or 'have you got owt for us?'

O

  • On the fiddleEngaged in petty cheating, fraud, or dishonest dealing, especially for small personal gain.
  • Over the moonDelighted; extremely pleased.
  • Over the topExcessive, exaggerated, or overdone.

P

  • Pull out all the stopsTo make a maximum, unrestrained effort.
  • Push the boat outTo spend generously, especially to celebrate something.

R

  • RadgieAn aggressive, hot-tempered person, or a fit of temper itself.
  • Raining cats and dogsRaining extremely heavily.
  • Read the riot actTo reprimand someone forcefully, warning them to stop misbehaving.
  • Rule of ThumbA broadly practical, approximate method or guideline rather than an exact, tested rule.

S

  • ScranFood, especially a meal.
  • Sent to CoventryTo be deliberately ignored or ostracised by a group.
  • Show a legGet out of bed; get moving.
  • Show your true coloursTo reveal your real character or intentions, especially after hiding them.
  • Sleep tightA wish for someone to sleep well.
  • Son of a GunA mild, often affectionate term for a rascal or someone remarkable.
  • Spill the BeansTo reveal a secret, often prematurely or accidentally.
  • Splice the mainbraceTo have a drink, especially to celebrate.
  • Steal someone's thunderTo take credit for someone else's idea, or to upstage them.

T

  • Ta-raGoodbye.
  • Take the biscuitTo be an extreme or most remarkable example of something, usually bad behaviour.
  • Taking coals to NewcastleDoing something pointless or superfluous.
  • Taking the mickeyTo mock, tease, or make fun of someone, often gently.
  • Talk the hind legs off a donkeyTo talk incessantly or excessively.
  • That's not cricketThat's not fair or proper conduct.
  • The full MontyThe whole thing; everything, with nothing left out.
  • The Green-Eyed MonsterJealousy, personified as a destructive force.
  • Three sheets to the windVery drunk.
  • Toe the lineTo conform to a rule or standard.
  • Turn a blind eyeTo deliberately ignore something you know is happening.

U

  • Under the WeatherFeeling slightly unwell or in low spirits.

W

  • WazzockA foolish or annoying person, said with a certain amount of affection.
  • Well, I'll Go to the Foot of Our StairsAn exclamation of surprise or disbelief, roughly 'well, I never!'
  • White ElephantA possession that's costly or troublesome to maintain, disproportionate to its actual usefulness.
