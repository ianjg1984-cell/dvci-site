A-Z Index

Every idiom in the repository so far (235 and counting), listed alphabetically. Tap a letter to jump straight there.

A B C D E F G H I J K L M N O P R S T U V W

A

  • A cock and bull storyAn elaborate, implausible tale.
  • A Dog's BreakfastA complete mess or muddle.
  • A Feather in Your CapAn achievement or honour to be proud of.
  • A Flash in the PanSomething that shows early promise but quickly fails or fizzles out.
  • A Method to the MadnessA hidden purpose or logic behind behaviour that looks irrational.
  • A Shot in the ArmSomething that provides a sudden, welcome boost of energy, confidence, or momentum.
  • A skeleton in the cupboardA shameful secret kept hidden, usually within a family.
  • A Taste of Your Own MedicineTo be treated the way you have treated others, especially badly.
  • A Wolf in Sheep's ClothingSomeone or something dangerous that's disguised as harmless or friendly.
  • Achilles' HeelA single point of weakness in someone or something otherwise strong.
  • Actions Speak Louder Than WordsWhat people do matters more than what they say.
  • Add Insult to InjuryTo make an already bad situation worse.
  • Argy-bargyA heated argument or dispute.
  • As Right as RainIn perfect health or good order.
  • At a loose endHaving nothing to do; idle.
  • At LoggerheadsIn serious dispute or conflict with someone.
  • At sixes and sevensIn a state of confusion or disarray.
  • At the Drop of a HatImmediately, without any hesitation or need for persuasion.

B

  • Back to the Drawing BoardTo start over after a plan or attempt has failed.
  • BairnA child, especially a baby or young son or daughter.
  • Baker's DozenThirteen, one more than a standard dozen.
  • Barking MadCompletely crazy or irrational.
  • Barking Up the Wrong TreeTo pursue a mistaken assumption or the wrong course of action entirely.
  • Batten down the hatchesTo prepare for a difficulty or crisis.
  • Beat around the bushTo avoid getting to the point, or to approach a difficult topic indirectly.
  • Bend Over BackwardsTo make an extreme effort to help or accommodate someone.
  • Between a Rock and a Hard PlaceCaught in a difficult situation where every available option is bad.
  • Between the devil and the deep blue seaStuck between two equally bad or difficult choices.
  • Beyond the paleOutside the bounds of acceptable behaviour.
  • Birds of a Feather Flock TogetherPeople with similar interests or character tend to associate with each other.
  • Bite Off More Than You Can ChewTo take on more responsibility or work than you can actually handle.
  • Bite the bulletTo face an unpleasant or painful situation with courage.
  • Bite your tongueTo stop yourself from saying something, especially something you might regret.
  • Bits and bobsA miscellaneous collection of small items.
  • Bob's your uncleThere you go; it's as simple as that.
  • Bog standardOrdinary, basic, without any special features.
  • BostinExcellent; great.
  • Break a LegA traditional way to wish a performer good luck right before they go on stage.
  • Break the iceTo ease initial social tension or awkwardness, especially between strangers.
  • Bricking itTo be extremely frightened or nervous.
  • Burn the Midnight OilTo work or study late into the night.
  • Burn Your BridgesTo destroy any possibility of retreating or going back to a previous situation.
  • Burning the Candle at Both EndsExhausting yourself by overworking, especially by staying up late and rising early.
  • Burst Your BubbleTo shatter someone's illusions, hopes, or fantasies.
  • Bury the HatchetTo formally end a conflict and restore peace.
  • By hook or by crookBy whatever means necessary, fair or otherwise.
  • By the Skin of Your TeethTo narrowly escape or achieve something, by the smallest possible margin.

C

  • Call It a DayTo stop working or doing something, usually because enough has been done for now.
  • Cast the First StoneA warning against judging or condemning others when you have faults of your own.
  • Cat Got Your Tongue?A question asked to someone who has suddenly gone quiet or is unable to speak.
  • Catch-22A no-win situation created by contradictory rules, where the only solution is blocked by the problem itself.
  • Caught red-handedCaught in the act of doing something wrong.
  • Cheesed OffAnnoyed, fed up, or irritated.
  • Chip on Your ShoulderTo carry a grudge or a persistent, defensive resentment, as if spoiling for a fight.
  • Chuffed to bitsExtremely pleased or delighted.
  • Close, But No CigarA near success that still falls short of actually winning or succeeding.
  • Cock-a-HoopExtremely and openly pleased, especially about a success.
  • CodswallopNonsense; rubbish talk.
  • Cold Enough to Freeze the Balls Off a Brass MonkeyAn exaggerated way of saying it's extremely cold.
  • Cold TurkeyTo stop a habit or addiction abruptly and completely, without weaning off gradually.
  • Cost a bombTo be very expensive.
  • Cost an Arm and a LegTo be extremely expensive.
  • Cry Over Spilled MilkTo waste time being upset about something that can't be undone.
  • Cry WolfTo raise a false alarm, especially repeatedly, so that a real warning later gets ignored.
  • Curiosity Killed the CatA warning that being too inquisitive can lead to trouble.
  • Curry favourTo ingratiate yourself with someone through flattery, especially for personal gain.
  • Cut and runTo leave quickly, especially to escape a difficult situation.
  • Cut CornersTo do something in the easiest or cheapest way, at the expense of quality.
  • Cut the MustardTo succeed, or to meet the required standard.
  • Cut to the ChaseTo get straight to the point, skipping unnecessary detail.
  • CwtchA hug or cuddle that carries a sense of safety and warmth, or a small cupboard.

D

  • Devil's AdvocateSomeone who argues an opposing position, often just to test an idea.
  • DiehardSomeone stubbornly loyal to a cause or belief, refusing to give up even against the odds.
  • Don't Judge a Book by Its CoverDon't form an opinion about something or someone based only on outward appearance.
  • Don't Put All Your Eggs in One BasketDon't risk everything on a single plan, investment, or option.
  • Donkey's yearsA very long time.
  • Dressed to the NinesDressed extremely smartly or elaborately, making a real effort.

E

  • Eat Humble PieTo be forced to admit you were wrong and apologise, often humiliatingly.
  • Elbow greaseHard physical effort, especially manual scrubbing or polishing.
  • Elbow roomEnough space to move or act freely.
  • Every Cloud Has a Silver LiningEven a difficult situation contains some hidden benefit or hope.
  • Ey upHello; also used as an exclamation of surprise or to draw attention.

F

  • Face the MusicTo accept the unpleasant consequences of your actions.
  • Fair clemmedVery hungry.
  • Fit as a fiddleIn excellent health or condition.

G

  • GadgieA man, especially an older bloke, sometimes used for any adult male.
  • Gan cannyTake care; go carefully.
  • Get a Second WindTo recover energy and enthusiasm after a period of tiredness.
  • Get Cold FeetTo lose your nerve and back out of a plan or commitment at the last moment.
  • GinnelA narrow alleyway or passage running between buildings, usually terraced houses.
  • Give it some wellyTo put in more effort or power, especially when driving or exerting physical force.
  • Give someone the cold shoulderTo deliberately treat someone with unfriendly indifference.
  • Give Up the GhostTo die, or (of a machine or object) to stop working entirely.
  • Go Down in FlamesTo fail dramatically and completely.
  • Go the Extra MileTo make a greater effort than is expected or required.
  • Go the Whole HogTo do something completely and thoroughly, holding nothing back.
  • Gone for a BurtonLost, broken, ruined, or (originally) killed.
  • Gordon Bennett!An exclamation of surprise, frustration, or exasperation.

H

  • Hair of the DogA small amount of alcohol taken to cure a hangover.
  • Hard and Fast RulesRules that are strict, fixed, and not open to exceptions.
  • Have a chin wagTo have a chat, especially a long or gossipy one.
  • Have an Axe to GrindTo have a hidden, self-interested motive behind your actions or opinions.
  • Head Over HeelsCompletely and often suddenly in love, or in a state of reckless enthusiasm.
  • Hit Below the BeltTo act unfairly, cruelly, or without regard for accepted standards of decency.
  • Hit the Nail on the HeadTo describe or identify something with complete accuracy.
  • Hit the RoadTo leave, or set off on a journey.
  • Hit the SackTo go to bed.
  • Hoist by Your Own PetardTo be undone by your own scheme or trick.
  • Hold Your HorsesWait, slow down, or be patient.

I

  • I don't like the cut of your jibTo dislike (or like) someone based on their general appearance or manner.
  • In a pickleIn a difficult, awkward, or messy situation.
  • In the LimelightAt the centre of public attention.
  • In the Nick of TimeAt the last possible moment; just in time.
  • In the Same BoatFacing the same difficult situation or circumstances as someone else.
  • It Takes Two to TangoBoth people involved share responsibility for a situation, especially a conflict.

J

  • Jack of All Trades, Master of NoneSomeone reasonably capable at many different things, but not a true expert in any single one.
  • JiggeredUtterly exhausted, or, as an exclamation ('well, I'll be jiggered!'), astonished.
  • Jump on the BandwagonTo join a popular activity, movement, or trend.
  • Jump the GunTo act prematurely, before the proper or agreed time.

K

  • Keep an Eye OnTo watch something or someone closely.
  • Keep Your Chin UpStay positive and optimistic, even in a difficult situation.
  • Kick the bucketTo die.
  • Kill Two Birds With One StoneTo achieve two goals with a single action.
  • Know the RopesTo understand how something works, especially through experience.
  • Know Your OnionsTo be knowledgeable and experienced about a subject.

L

  • Let Sleeping Dogs LieDon't disturb a settled situation, or you may provoke new trouble.
  • Let the cat out of the bagTo reveal a secret, often accidentally.
  • Let the Chips Fall Where They MayTo accept whatever outcome results from doing the right thing, without trying to control it.
  • Long in the ToothOld, or past one's prime.
  • Loose CannonAn unpredictable person prone to causing damage if left unchecked.

M

  • Mad as a hatterCompletely crazy or eccentric.
  • Mad as a March HareCompletely wild, crazy, or eccentric.
  • Make a Long Story ShortTo summarise or skip ahead to the point.
  • MardySulky, moody, or irritable.
  • Mind your Ps and QsBehave properly; be on your best manners.
  • Miss the BoatTo miss an opportunity, especially through delay.
  • MitherTo pester, nag, or make an unnecessary fuss.
  • Mum's the WordKeep quiet; don't reveal a secret.

N

  • Neck and NeckVery closely matched in a competition, with no clear leader.
  • NeshOverly sensitive to cold weather; soft or feeble.
  • No Pain, No GainWorthwhile results require difficult or uncomfortable effort.
  • No room to swing a catAn extremely small or cramped space.
  • Not the full shillingNot very intelligent, or not quite right in the head.
  • Nowt and Owt"Nowt" means nothing and "owt" means anything, as in 'there's nowt so queer as folk' or 'have you got owt for us?'

O

  • Off the CuffSaid or done without preparation; improvised on the spot.
  • On Cloud NineExtremely happy; euphoric.
  • On the BallAlert, competent, and quick to respond.
  • On the fiddleEngaged in petty cheating, fraud, or dishonest dealing, especially for small personal gain.
  • Once Bitten, Twice ShyA bad experience makes you cautious about repeating it.
  • Once in a Blue MoonVery rarely.
  • Out of the BlueSuddenly and without warning.
  • Over the moonDelighted; extremely pleased.
  • Over the topExcessive, exaggerated, or overdone.

P

  • Paint the Town RedTo go out and celebrate in a wild, unrestrained way.
  • Pandora's BoxAn action that seems small or harmless but unleashes a great many unforeseen problems.
  • Pie in the SkyAn unrealistic, overly optimistic promise or plan that will probably never happen.
  • Piece of CakeSomething very easy to do.
  • Play It by EarTo improvise or decide how to act as a situation unfolds, rather than planning in advance.
  • Pull No PunchesTo speak or act with complete honesty and force, holding nothing back.
  • Pull out all the stopsTo make a maximum, unrestrained effort.
  • Pull Your LegTo tease someone, especially by telling them something untrue as a joke.
  • Push the boat outTo spend generously, especially to celebrate something.
  • Put a Sock in ItBe quiet; stop talking, singing, or making noise.
  • Put Your Foot DownTo assert authority firmly, refusing to allow something.

R

  • RadgieAn aggressive, hot-tempered person, or a fit of temper itself.
  • Raining cats and dogsRaining extremely heavily.
  • Read the riot actTo reprimand someone forcefully, warning them to stop misbehaving.
  • Red HerringA misleading clue or distraction from the real issue.
  • Red TapeExcessive, rigid bureaucracy and official procedure.
  • Riding ShotgunSitting in the front passenger seat of a vehicle, or more broadly, providing support or protection alongside someone.
  • Rob Peter to Pay PaulTo solve one financial problem by creating another, usually by taking from one source to cover a different debt.
  • Rule of ThumbA broadly practical, approximate method or guideline rather than an exact, tested rule.
  • Rule the RoostTo be the dominant, controlling figure in a household or group.
  • Run the GauntletTo face a barrage of criticism, danger, or difficulty from multiple directions at once.

S

  • Salt of the EarthA person or group considered genuine, dependable, and fundamentally decent, often in an unpretentious, working-class sense.
  • Saved by the BellRescued from a difficult situation at the very last moment.
  • ScranFood, especially a meal.
  • See Eye to EyeTo agree completely with someone.
  • Sent to CoventryTo be deliberately ignored or ostracised by a group.
  • Show a legGet out of bed; get moving.
  • Show your true coloursTo reveal your real character or intentions, especially after hiding them.
  • Sit on the FenceTo avoid committing to either side of a decision or argument.
  • Sleep tightA wish for someone to sleep well.
  • Son of a GunA mild, often affectionate term for a rascal or someone remarkable.
  • Sour GrapesDisparaging something you couldn't have or achieve, to make yourself feel better about missing out.
  • Spick and SpanNeat, clean, and tidy.
  • Spill the BeansTo reveal a secret, often prematurely or accidentally.
  • Splice the mainbraceTo have a drink, especially to celebrate.
  • Steal someone's thunderTo take credit for someone else's idea, or to upstage them.
  • Steal the ShowTo attract all the attention, outshining everyone else in a performance or event.
  • Straight from the horse's mouthInformation from the most direct, authoritative source possible.

T

  • Ta-raGoodbye.
  • Take It on the ChinTo face hardship, criticism, or defeat with resilience and without complaint.
  • Take It With a Grain of SaltTo view a claim with healthy skepticism rather than accepting it fully.
  • Take the biscuitTo be an extreme or most remarkable example of something, usually bad behaviour.
  • Taking coals to NewcastleDoing something pointless or superfluous.
  • Taking the mickeyTo mock, tease, or make fun of someone, often gently.
  • Talk the hind legs off a donkeyTo talk incessantly or excessively.
  • That's not cricketThat's not fair or proper conduct.
  • The Apple of My EyeA person cherished above all others.
  • The Ball Is in Your CourtIt's now your turn to act or make a decision.
  • The Bee's KneesSomething or someone considered excellent, the best.
  • The Elephant in the RoomAn obvious problem or difficult topic that everyone is avoiding discussing.
  • The full MontyThe whole thing; everything, with nothing left out.
  • The Green-Eyed MonsterJealousy, personified as a destructive force.
  • The Last StrawThe final, seemingly small event that causes a much larger reaction after a long buildup.
  • The Life of RileyA carefree, comfortable existence, free from worry or hardship.
  • The Lion's ShareThe largest part of something, often unfairly so.
  • The Pen Is Mightier Than the SwordWords and ideas have more lasting power to influence people than violence or force.
  • The Pot Calling the Kettle BlackAccusing someone of a fault that you share yourself.
  • The Proof of the Pudding Is in the EatingThe real value or success of something can only be judged by trying it or seeing the results.
  • The Real McCoyThe genuine, authentic thing, not an imitation.
  • The Whole Kit and CaboodleEverything; the entire lot, with nothing left out.
  • The Whole Nine YardsEverything; the full amount, with nothing held back.
  • The Whole ShebangEverything; the entire thing, with all its parts included.
  • The World Is Your OysterYou have complete freedom and opportunity to make of life whatever you want.
  • The Writing on the WallA clear sign that something bad or disastrous is about to happen.
  • Three sheets to the windVery drunk.
  • Throw Caution to the WindTo act boldly, ignoring risk or restraint.
  • Throw Down the GauntletTo issue a direct, formal challenge.
  • Throw in the TowelTo give up or admit defeat.
  • Tickled PinkExtremely pleased or delighted.
  • Toe the lineTo conform to a rule or standard.
  • Turn a blind eyeTo deliberately ignore something you know is happening.
  • Turn Over a New LeafTo reform your behaviour and start afresh.

U

  • Under the WeatherFeeling slightly unwell or in low spirits.
  • Up to ScratchMeeting the required standard; good enough.

V

  • Vicious CircleA self-perpetuating negative situation, where one problem causes another that makes the first one worse.

W

  • Walking on EggshellsBeing extremely cautious not to upset or offend someone.
  • WazzockA foolish or annoying person, said with a certain amount of affection.
  • Well, I'll Go to the Foot of Our StairsAn exclamation of surprise or disbelief, roughly 'well, I never!'
  • Wet Behind the EarsYoung, naive, and inexperienced.
  • When Pigs FlyUsed to say something will never happen.
  • White ElephantA possession that's costly or troublesome to maintain, disproportionate to its actual usefulness.
  • Wild Goose ChaseA hopeless or fruitless pursuit of something unattainable.
